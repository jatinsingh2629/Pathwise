Why CBR → RBR Wins

CBR → RBR (Our System)
CBR first retrieves cases from real learners who succeeded in similar goals. RBR then applies pedagogical rules on that targeted subset — so rules refine an already-personalised candidate set.
  • Uses proven human learning paths
  • Rules applied to relevant modules only
  • Higher BERT semantic alignment
  • Fastest: processes small candidate set
RBR → CBR
Rules fire first on the entire module pool without any personalisation signal. CBR then tries to re-rank an already rule-filtered set — but loses the case-based context that makes retrieval meaningful.
  • Rules are generic — no learner context
  • CBR retrieval is diluted after filtering
  • Slower: RBR processes all modules
  • Lower personalisation alignment
⚡
CBR ∥ RBR (Parallel)
Both pipelines run independently and merge their outputs. The merger introduces incoherence — CBR and RBR modules may contradict each other in difficulty ordering and style.
  • Merge creates difficulty-ordering conflicts
  • Lower coherence and pedagogical score
  • Slowest: two full passes + merge cost
  • Confidence drops — two conflicting signals
📖 Supporting Literature
Aamodt & Plaza (1994) established that CBR's strength lies in retrieving contextually similar past solutions before adaptation — directly supporting our CBR→RBR ordering.
Watson (1997) demonstrated that case retrieval provides the most personalised starting point, with rules as a secondary refinement mechanism.
Kolodner (1993) showed that rule-first approaches sacrifice user-specificity because rules encode population-level knowledge, not individual similarity.
